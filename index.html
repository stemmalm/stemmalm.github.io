StemmaLM

Small language models raised from scratch, one generation teaching the next.

Each individual begins knowing nothing. It hears what adults said to real children, in order of the child’s age, and a snapshot is kept for every month of its life. Grown individuals copy what they heard and raise the next generation with their copy. The question at every stage is which properties of language grow out of this input alone, and which need something the input does not contain.

1 · architecture

Learners

A strain is the learner an individual is born as: a small GPT with a fixed shape, vocabulary and training rule. Individuals of one strain differ only in seed and input.

EN1 is the baseline. EN2 tests whether a larger learner of the same kind learns more; EN3–EN5 are subword learners large enough for the BabyLM corpora.

3 · training

Raising

Individuals raised on Ω live on a month clock. The adult speech recorded at each month of the child’s age is one segment, heard ten times before the next month begins, never going back. A snapshot is kept at the end of every month, so any individual can be tested, or talked to, at any age.

At 72 months some individuals go to school: children’s books from easy to hard, one level a year, up to 144 months. Clones of the same individuals hear the same books with 30% family talk mixed in. Individuals on the BabyLM roots are trained in ten parts instead and have no month clock.

4 · generations

Lineage

A grown individual copies, word by word, everything adults said in its own upbringing. At each word it lapses with probability 5% and writes the word it expects instead. The copy raises the next generation, which inherits the parent’s lapses along with the adults’ words. The name stemma comes from textual criticism: the family tree of a manuscript, read back from the errors each copy adds.

Every individual has a number and a full name that records how it came to be:

5 · how they are measured

Analysis

Every snapshot is looked at from four angles.

Speech

Prompted with held-out adult lines, each snapshot speaks a thousand utterances. Length, real-word share and errors are counted against real children of the same age.

Grammar check-up

Pairs of sentences that differ in one point of grammar: agreement, past tense, plurals, and 23 test types from Zorro. Which of the pair does the model prefer, month by month?

check-up →

Held-out loss

Bits per character on adult speech and books the individual never heard. It shows what is learnt and what is forgotten, as when school replaces family talk.

Inherited variants

6 · results

Findings

  1. It speaks like an adult before it speaks like a child.
  2. Word endings come early. Agreement stays shaky.
  3. A bigger learner, or more of the right sentences, did not teach more grammar.
  4. Books alone erase conversation. Three parts in ten of talk keep it.
  5. Children inherit the slips of the copy they heard.

All exploratory: three individuals per condition, not yet repeated on a fresh set. New observations go to the log first.
