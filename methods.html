Methods

How the models are made and how they are measured.

Learners · Input · Raising · Copying · Names · Measures

Learners

A strain is the learner a model is born as: a small GPT with a fixed shape, vocabulary and training rule. Models of one strain differ only in seed and input.

StrainLearnerModels

EN1 is the baseline. EN2 tests whether a larger learner of the same kind learns more. EN3–EN5 are subword learners large enough for the BabyLM corpora.

Input

What a model hears is its upbringing. Every upbringing starts from one of three roots, and a condition changes one thing in a root: doubling a kind of sentence, rewriting the metaphors, adding books.

Raising

Models raised on Ω live on a month clock. The adult speech recorded at each month of the child’s age is one segment, heard ten times before the next month begins, never going back. A snapshot is kept at the end of every month, so any model can be tested, or talked to, at any age.

At 72 months some models go to school: children’s books from easy to hard, one level a year, up to 144 months. Clones of the same models hear the same books with 30% family talk mixed in. Models on the BabyLM roots are trained in parts instead and have no month clock.

Copying

A grown model copies, word by word, everything adults said in its own upbringing. At each word it lapses with probability 5% and writes the word it expects instead. The copy raises the next generation, which inherits the parent’s lapses along with the adults’ words. The name stemma comes from textual criticism: the family tree of a manuscript, read back from the errors each copy adds.

Measures

Speech
Prompted with held-out adult lines, each snapshot speaks a thousand utterances. Length, real-word share and errors are counted against real children of the same age.
Grammar check-up
Pairs of sentences that differ in one point of grammar: agreement, past tense, plurals, and 23 test types from Zorro. Which of the pair does the model prefer, month by month?
Held-out loss
Bits per character on adult speech and books the model never heard. It shows what is learnt and what is forgotten, as when school replaces family talk.
Inherited variants
Word pairs a parent introduced by lapsing while copying. Do its children say them more than children raised on another parent’s copy? The copies can be read side by side in the collation.
